## Patterns

The track is accurate and current on the whole: product names and numbers (Autopilot device preparation limits, Frontline renamed Flex, retired Require approved client app grant control, Intune advanced capabilities in E3/E5 from July 2026, 26H2 baselines, quiet time policy types, Cloud PC roles and image rules) all checked out against Learn. The real problems were presentational: about 60 definitions began with, or contained, the term itself in a form the blanking did not catch (singular versus plural, a dropped "Windows" or "Microsoft", an acronym expansion), a handful of bracketed or listed fronts produced double blanks or left a second name visible (f65, f66, f124, f9115, f9201), and many "A vs B" and list fronts (f2, f4, f9, f13, f14, f50, f55, f58, f63, f74, f81 and similar) name both sides in the definition by design; those were left alone. A few facts had slipped with time (cleanup rules now hide rather than delete, Windows settings backup default-on from 26H2, FOTA and Tunnel for MAM licensing, 24H2 as a target version) and the later 9xxx series repeated earlier ideas (q9203 with q21, q9004 with tf7, f22 and f9203 with f9201). Questions are well built: keys and explanations agree, no letter references, multi-select counts are right; the main defect was correct options that were conspicuously longest in the newer series, and the mad libs gave answers away through "a/an".
