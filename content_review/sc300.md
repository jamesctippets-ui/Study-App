## Patterns
The track was accurate and the questions were sound: keys were right, explanations agreed with them, and no stem contradicted itself. The recurring defect was the new definition-first prompt: about 60 definitions repeated the term in slightly different words, so nothing was blanked, and nearly every "A vs. B" or list card named all its parts in the definition. Four cards blanked the term two or three times in one sentence. The stale or over-claimed facts were all about things Microsoft is retiring or renaming (SMS/voice, the SSPR registration setting, what the registration campaign replaces, token protection scope). The newer Global Secure Access and Defender for Cloud Apps questions often gave the key away by length; those were evened out. Technical note for maintainers: lesson key terms link to cards by exact front, or by the phrase appearing in a card's front, definition or example, so a front or definition rewrite can silently break a lesson link (this happened for five terms and was fixed).
